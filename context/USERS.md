# USERS
Status: ACTIVE.
## Profile 1: Student comic creator
Evidence status: inferred from my experience and reported project context.
A student comic creator writes, draws, and promotes independent comic work while completing coursework. They need to see what production work is planned, what is complete, and which tasks may compete with academic commitments. Their current approach may be a mixture of memory, calendar entries, notes, and informal deadlines.
The current approach makes it difficult to see all production tasks together or notice when a release plan is becoming unrealistic. A useful tool should make work visible without forcing every creative activity into a rigid prediction. The creator needs to revise plans when a task takes longer than expected and should remain responsible for artistic decisions.
## Profile 2: Creative collaborator
Evidence status: assumed and informed by the collaborative implications of the project.
A creative collaborator may help with lettering, editing, illustration, promotion, or release preparation. They need a clear view of task names, dates, and statuses so they can understand what work is ready, active, or unfinished.
Their current approach may rely on messages, separate spreadsheets, or verbal updates. That approach can make ownership and progress unclear. This HW3 version does not implement multi-user collaboration, but its task structure is intended to remain understandable if collaboration is added later.
## Research notes
The Dawud Anyabwile interview provided reported insight about authenticity, deliberate craft, and building an audience around work that has personal value. The Kevin Coxe interview provided reported insight about measuring progress, testing assumptions, and revising a plan when the original bottleneck is wrong.
These two interviews do not establish population prevalence. They inform design hypotheses and the boundaries of the feature.
